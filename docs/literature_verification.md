## Entries

**ECB Working Paper 2968.** López, L., F. Odendahl, S. Párraga Rodríguez and E. Silgado-Gómez (2024), "The pass-through to inflation of gas price shocks", ECB Working Paper 2968, August 2024 (https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp2968~e514c92723.en.pdf). A Bayesian structural VAR for the euro area and its four largest economies, with a DSGE model for interpretation. The paper attributes cross-country differences partly to "variation in the regulation of retail energy prices" (p. 2) and states that "France's highly regulated energy markets dampen the impact", while Spain's electricity market regulation "allows for a faster transmission of prices from wholesale to retail" (pp. 3, 5). The design's description holds.

**European Commission Discussion Paper 197.** Coutinho, L. and M. Licchetta (2023), "Inflation Differentials in the Euro Area at the Time of High Energy Prices", European Economy Discussion Paper 197, November 2023 (https://economy-finance.ec.europa.eu/publications/inflation-differentials-euro-area-time-high-energy-prices_en). A common factor, mainly energy and food prices, explains about half of the 2022 rise in euro-area inflation, up to two thirds without the lagged dependent variable. The paper links persistence to "a relatively long pass-through for the energy shock, related to the staggered nature of supply contracts and price setting". The full text (24 pages) never uses the word "regulated"; it mentions energy price caps and freezes (France, Malta) as inflation-suppressing fiscal measures that complicate its estimates (pp. 11, 15). Correct wording: "links the persistence of euro-area inflation differentials in 2022 to slow pass-through of energy costs through staggered contracts and price setting, and notes that price caps and freezes (France, Malta) held down measured inflation."

**Erceg, Kolasa, Lindé and Pescatori (2024).** "Can Energy Subsidies Help Slay Inflation?", IMF Working Paper 2024/081 and CEPR Discussion Paper 19085 (https://ideas.repec.org/p/imf/imfwpa/2024-081.html). The abstract states that "a consumer subsidy may be counterproductive, especially as an inflation-fighting tool, when applied globally or in a segmented market, at least under empirically plausible conditions about wage-setting", that there is "more scope for energy subsidies to reduce core inflation and stimulate demand if introduced by a small group of countries", and that targeted transfers "transmit less to core inflation". The design's statement that subsidies "lower headline inflation at first but can make it more persistent" is not in the abstract; the IMF full text could not be retrieved and was not read. Correct wording: "argue that consumer energy subsidies may be counterproductive as an inflation-fighting tool when adopted widely, with more scope to lower core inflation when only a few countries use them."

**Imam and Poghosyan (2026).** Imam, P. A. and T. Poghosyan (2026), "One Global Shock, Many Inflation Paths: Explaining Post-COVID Inflation Divergence", IMF Working Paper 2026/103, May 2026 (https://ideas.repec.org/p/imf/imfwpa/2026-103.html). Cross-country regressions for 130 economies and local projections for 70. The abstract reports that pass-through from energy prices to headline inflation "intensified markedly in the post-COVID period, particularly in emerging markets, in non-inflation-targeting regimes, and in countries that did not expand fossil fuel subsidies", and that the association with domestic policy responses is weak and complicated by endogeneity. Correct wording: "find that pass-through from energy prices to headline inflation rose less after COVID in countries that expanded fossil fuel subsidies."

**Krebs and Weber (2024).** Krebs, T. and I. M. Weber (2024), "Can Price Controls Be Optimal? The Economics of the Energy Shock in Germany", IZA Discussion Paper 17043, June 2024 (https://docs.iza.org/dp17043.pdf). The paper shows in a production model with an energy sector "that price controls are socially optimal whenever self-fulfilling expectations generate endogenous price uncertainty in the wake of an energy shock" (p. 3). The design's description holds.

**Xie and Huang (2026).** The only source found is a blog post of 16 February 2026 that reposts an abstract titled "Energy price shocks and inflation: The cross-country comparison of energy price management systems" by Li Xie and Zhisheng Huang (https://unassumingeconomist.com/?p=11522). The abstract describes a DSGE model comparing the energy price management systems of developed countries and China and finds China's system more effective at limiting the inflationary effect of energy price shocks. No journal, working-paper series, year or DOI was found. Not to be cited until the publication is located.

**Banque de France bulletin.** Lemoine, M., A. Petronevich and A. Zhutova (2024), "Energy tariff shield in France: what is the outcome?", Bulletin de la Banque de France 253/4, July 2024 (https://ideas.repec.org/a/bfr/bullbf/202425304.html). Simulations with the FR-BDF model. The abstract reports that the shield lowered inflation by a cumulative 2.2 percentage points over 2022-23 and that "in the event of a more persistent shock, a tariff shield would only delay the inevitable rise in inflation, unless it were kept in place over the longer term at a prohibitive fiscal cost". The cumulative effect over 2022 to 2025 of about −0.4 points (−2.6 in 2022, then +0.4, +1.0 and +0.8) comes from the bulletin's table as quoted in a search excerpt; the Banque de France PDF could not be retrieved. Check the table before citing that figure.

**INSEE.** Insee Analyses n° 75 (September 2022), on the effect of the tariff shield (https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/6524161): the rise in energy prices added 3.1 points of inflation between the second quarter of 2021 and the second quarter of 2022, against 6.2 points without the shield. The design's statement that INSEE estimates a larger short-run effect than the Banque de France holds.

**EsadeEcPol.** "Estimando el efecto del tope al precio del gas", EsadeEcPol, January 2023 (https://www.esade.edu/ecpol/es/publicaciones/estimando-el-efecto-del-tope-al-precio-del-gas/). Press coverage reports an estimate that Spain's gas price cap for power generation lowered 2022 inflation by about 0.3 to 0.4 points. The EsadeEcPol page could not be retrieved, so the note itself was not read. Read it before citing a figure.

**Unidentified IMF working paper.** The design says an IMF working paper "could not account for price controls for lack of such data" and marks the paper as to be identified. Searches of IMF working papers on European energy pass-through (including WP/22/152 and WP/22/262) did not find this statement. Remove the claim unless the paper is found.
